The concept

Live road reports from riders, delivered in each rider's language

RiderSafe AI is a proposed navigation and safety platform. Riders quickly report changing road conditions such as hazards, blocked cycling lanes and congestion. Reports are tied to a location, shared with nearby riders and checked by the community, then used for timely visual and audio alerts and safer route suggestions.

Value proposition
“RiderSafe AI helps international food-delivery riders navigate Melbourne more safely by turning real-time rider hazard reports into multilingual alerts and route updates.”

The design keeps phone interaction to a minimum while riders are moving, so a report takes one tap and an alert can be heard rather than read.

Problem and primary user

International food-delivery riders in MelbourneRiding bicycles, e-bikes and motorbikes through the CBD
  • Often unfamiliar with local road conditions
  • May face language barriers with English-only information
  • Need timely road information while completing deliveries

Design challenge: the problem is not simply navigation. It is how to give riders specific, changing road-condition information without creating extra distraction.

Core features

Three report types, plus audio alerts and community checks

The three colour-coded report types cover what riders meet on the road. Multilingual audio and community verification keep that information usable and accurate.

How the system works

From one tap to a safer route

Every report follows the same seven steps, so the shared map stays current for every rider.

  1. 1

    Rider detects a road issue

  2. 2

    Taps one quick-report button

  3. 3

    Location and time are recorded

  4. 4

    Report enters the shared hazard map

  5. 5

    Nearby riders receive an alert

  6. 6

    Other riders confirm or clear it

  7. 7

    A safer alternative route is recommended

Confirmations keep a report on the map. “Clear now” removes it, so outdated information doesn't linger.

    Pilot and testing

    Start small, in Melbourne CBD

    The first pilot is limited to the CBD instead of a city-wide launch, so the team can watch how riders actually use the reports and alerts.

    Proposed Melbourne CBD pilot

    Pilot zone · Hoddle Grid
    The pilot tests whether riders
    • Understand the interface
    • Use the reporting system
    • Trust community-generated information
    • Notice the alerts
    • Make appropriate route decisions after an alert

    Key hypothesis to test

    Can community-generated, real-time hazard information help international food-delivery riders make better-informed and potentially safer route decisions?

    Claims we are not making

    The prototype does not claim to have reduced crashes or proven safety outcomes. The pilot is designed to test usability, rider behaviour, trust, perceived safety and route decisions.
